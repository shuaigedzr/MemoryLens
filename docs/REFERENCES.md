Verification is not a claim to have downloaded and reread every paper's full text during this build. A1's original five references and limitations are retained in SOURCE_AUDIT.md; only cited items belong in the report bibliography.

1. Amershi, S., Weld, D., Vorvoreanu, M., Fourney, A., Nushi, B., Collisson, P., Suh, J., Iqbal, S., Bennett, P.N., Inkpen, K., Teevan, J., Kikin-Gil, R. and Horvitz, E. (2019) 'Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction', CHI '19, pp. 1–13. https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300233. Author-hosted camera-ready paper: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf. Table 1 supports G9 correction, G10 disambiguation, G11 explanations, G16 consequences. These are design guidance, not proof this app is usable.
2. Jiang, Z., Zheng, C., Chen, H. and Chen, L. (2026) 'RECALLbot: Designing Agentic Memory and Reciprocal Disclosure for Human–Chatbot Relationships', CHI '26, article 795, pp. 1–20. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790714. Publisher indexed abstract describes N=40, two-week comparison of multiple changed features. Direct subsequent page fetch returned 403; no additional full-text claim is based on that failed fetch. Does not isolate explanation effects.
3. Richards, D. and Bransky, K. (2014) 'ForgetMeNot: What and how users expect intelligent virtual agents to recall and forget personal conversational content', International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 72(5), pp. 460–476. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2014.01.005. Verified bibliographic details and incorrect-recall/frustration finding in the author's Macquarie record: https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/forgetmenot-what-and-how-users-expect-intelligent-virtual-agents-/ . Real-estate-agent context differs from study planning.
4. Wu, D., Wang, H., Yu, W., Zhang, Y., Chang, K.-W. and Yu, D. (2025) 'LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory', ICLR. https://openreview.net/forum?id=pZiyCaVuti. Verified author paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813 and official ICLR record. Five abilities include updating, temporal reasoning and abstention; benchmark answers do not measure user understanding of an explanation. MemoryLens does not run this benchmark.
5. Yan, Z., Fischer, J.E. and Clos, J. (2025) 'Remembering Things Makes Chatbots Sound Smarter, but Less Trustworthy - A Pilot Study', CUI '25, article 23, pp. 1–8. https://doi.org/10.1145/3719160.3737617. Official conference abstract: https://cui.acm.org/2025/proceedings/ . It compares no memory, memory and memory with visualisation, assessing likeability/intelligence/safety. Preliminary perceptions are not direct evidence of correction performance.
6. Yuan, R., Sun, S., Li, Y., Wang, Z., Cao, Z. and Li, W. (2025) 'Personalized Large Language Model Assistant with Evolving Conditional Memory', COLING, pp. 3764–3777. https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.254/ . Official ACL record verifies authors/pages and memory-bank/retrieval framework. MemoryLens uses explicit records but does not implement their method or claim comparable performance.
7. University of Birmingham (2026a) Conversational Interfaces. Intelligent Interactive Systems, W03 L07, teaching slides, pp. 7–16. Supports mixed initiative, grounding, clarify/confirm/correct/decline, and a form/state display rather than unrestricted chat.
8. University of Birmingham (2026c) Context Engineering and Memory. Intelligent Interactive Systems, W04 L10, teaching slides, pp. 8–15. Supports recording selected context; inspectable, editable, forgettable records; dated evidence and stale-memory repair.
9. University of Birmingham (2026d) Retrieval, Grounding and Provenance. Intelligent Interactive Systems, W04 L11, teaching slides, pp. 7–13. Supports relevance versus correctness, specific visible source excerpts, dates and no-answer behaviour.
10. University of Birmingham (2026e) Tools, Connectors and the Boundary. Intelligent Interactive Systems, W04 L12, teaching slides, pp. 8, 12–16. Supports checked mutation outcomes, confirmations and undo; no runtime tool calling is claimed here.
11. University of Birmingham (2026b) Prompting as Interaction. Intelligent Interactive Systems, W03 L09, teaching slides, pp. 4, 7–10. Supports Norman's action cycle, shape versus truth, and fixed-case testing; this prototype does not prompt an LLM.
